Tone-shaped color operators

Color computes adjustment coefficients from the post-Tone appearance and applies them to fixed incoming color attributes. Lightness and chroma each receive a driver-weighted coefficient; a tonal web shapes hue and chroma, and selective cone Adaptance moves neutral destinations. With the reference’s default hk_guard=True, H–K repair follows Adaptance and precedes the web’s final common Scale. The explicit hk_guard=False mode is an unrepaired diagnostic path and is outside the guard guarantees below.

Shape hue and chroma with the tonal web

The web is a four-row, six-handle field. Each of the six handles is an actual Rec.709 primary ray — red, yellow, green, cyan, blue and magenta — scaled to the row’s tonal height. The four rows are the tonal anchors Shadows, Midtones, White and Highlights. Between rows, handle angles interpolate directly and radii interpolate in log space.

Each row stores three relative controls per handle:

log_chroma of target radius over source radius, before adaptation and scale

All three arrays are zero at reset, which is the identity. The web is therefore additive in intent but multiplicative in effect, and no single scalar reports its strength.

Compose the operators

The evaluation order is fixed:

  1. The lightness and chroma coefficient rows above.
  2. The web’s hue warp and radial chroma edit, at fixed signed , carrying the achromatic energy.
  3. Selective cone Adaptance, changing and the signed achromatic energy.
  4. With the default guard enabled, H–K repair uses the frozen post-Tone reference energy on the nonnegative- branch. Negative takes the signed bypass; hk_guard=False skips repair for diagnostics.
  5. The web’s final positive scale , selected from the pre-Adaptance web state: multiply by and the resulting carried energy by .

The final scale comes last and is chosen before Adaptance and repair, so those operators cannot change its selection. It preserves wherever that ratio is defined. With the default guard enabled, repair makes energy nonnegative for requests with , and positive Scale preserves its sign.

The six source rays begin with positive energy, but increasing chroma at fixed signed can cross into negative energy; Adaptance can also change the energy. In the earlier Scale-before-repair order, the guard’s balance could change as Scale varied because its source reference energy stayed fixed. Repairing the request first removes that interaction from final Scale.

The angular map preserves cyclic hue order at fixed chroma and tonal selector. This does not establish global hue ordering or injectivity for the complete color-dependent grade, which includes Adaptance. Appearance Stack handoffs carry the post-policy signed state after Scale. With the default guard enabled, nonnegative- requests are repaired, while negative- requests bypass repair; Scale multiplies either carried energy by , preserving its sign. RGB cube projection happens only at export.

Legacy controls

Earlier releases exposed three controls that this part does not describe, because the reference does not implement them:

  • Hue Concentration, an orientation-preserving circle map that gathers hues toward a chosen direction with no change to or .
  • Adaptance Strength and Adaptance Fit, a scalar strength inside a chroma-protection envelope with .
  • Global low-chroma and high-chroma Hue, two rotation coefficients weighted by the and drivers.

The 1.23.2 DCTL and Blender release implement all three, and their saved Grades use them. The reference replaced them with the web and the neutral destinations above, which is why saved settings from one are not reinterpreted as the other. Their full equations remain in the archived release documentation and in the retained DCTL oracle.

The 1.23.2 Concentration operator. Positive concentration gathers nearby hues and expands the opposite side; the local spacing ratio stays positive, preserving its hue order. The reference replaces this operator with the web's angular map.
